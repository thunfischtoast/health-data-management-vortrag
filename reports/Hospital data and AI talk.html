What the evidence says about your talk

The story holds, and the local evidence for it is better than expected. Eight points carry the talk. Click any highlighted statement to see its source, its quote and how it was checked.

  1. Your opening problem is documented at UKM. MeDIC writes that the ORBIS study module searches structured fields and free text, but cannot search inside PDF documents. In a 2026 liver cancer study, UKM radiology pulled reports out of ORBIS as PDF files and merged them into one file per patient before a model could read them.
  2. The vendor says it too. Dedalus wrote in October 2026 that using ORBIS still requires navigating modules, views and forms. Its chief medical officer said that such systems often only imitate the paper chart. These quotes let you be openly critical on solid ground.
  3. Drop the 80% figure. No traceable measurement stands behind it. Use a measured one: in 1.8 million Dutch GP records, 13% of the concepts in free text had a structured counterpart.
  4. Today's data is already doubtful. In ten German hospitals about a third of severe sepsis cases had a sepsis code. Copying values by hand from records into a study has a pooled error rate of 6.6%. AI has to be compared with imperfect humans.
  5. Extraction works when the fact is written down. Local models reached human level for "is there a metastasis?" but fell short for "did the tumour respond?". Person plus model was the best combination: 68% to 89% correct, 9.4 to 3.6 minutes per patient, in a small study.
  6. A chain of proof helps and can also mislead. Clinicians with a source for each claim were 37.6% faster at non-inferior quality. When a citation seemed to support a wrong answer, doctors dropped their own correct answer in 15 of 23 cases. A link shows where a value comes from. It proves neither the interpretation nor completeness.
  7. General summaries disappoint so far. A 2026 trial of Epic's chart summary found less perceived load, no time saved, and falling use. Concrete questions seem to profit more than unrequested summaries. That comparison is my inference across studies, and I found no direct test of it.
  8. The closing argument is concrete. From 2027, ePA data are to go to the national research data centre in structured form only. PDF findings will not be passed on.

5 minStatus quo: we store documents, we need answers

Message. Hospital systems digitised the paper chart. Cases contain documents, and questions cut across all of them.

Evidence, local. No search inside PDFs in the UKM study module. Extracts arrive as a printout or Excel sheet. 8,700 GB and more than 40 clinics (2020).

Evidence, general. In Dresden's ORBIS, 47.7% of 1.77 million prescriptions were structured, and text processing raised this to 85.2%. At Penn half of all note text was copied. In Erlangen, collecting 251 cases by hand took 668 hours against about 35 with a data warehouse.

Why not more forms? Expect this question. In Norway doctors kept preferring free text, and structured reports do not always win. RACOON's first template had 67 items and needed more time than routine reading. This leads to the next section: let people write, and let the machine structure.

Slide. A folder tree (case, document, form) that rearranges into a timeline for one patient.

5 minNew methods: four verbs

Message. Extract, classify, summarise, search by meaning. One intuitive example each.

Extract. Münster example: 14 heart values from 497 German cardiac MRI reports, without fine-tuning.

Classify. On German chest X-ray reports, an open model without training reached 91.6%, the rule-based system 74.8% (macro F1, Bonn).

Search by meaning. Atemnot, Kurzatmigkeit, dyspnea, SOB are one symptom. Keyword search scores 84 on identical words and 45 on synonyms. The best semantic search scores 84 on synonyms.

Summarise. Short texts work (mostly rated equal to or better than experts, 2024). In daily use the main fault is what is left out: 25 of 100 reviewed summaries had omissions, 2 had invented content.

Limit, as a picture. pN1 and Pn1 differ by capital letters and mean lymph nodes versus nerves.

Local is possible. Bonn runs an open model on its own server, approved for patient data.

Slide. One invented letter stays on screen. Each verb lights up another part of it.

Likely questions, short answers

May I put patient data into ChatGPT or UniGPT?

Not into public services. A Dutch practice employee's chatbot use was reported as a data breach. UniGPT allows class V3 in local models and never V4. The class of patient data is in an internal handbook, so check it before the talk. Düsseldorf prohibits personal data in KI:connect. Running locally is no clearance by itself.

Which law allows research on our own patients' data?

Section 6 GDNG allows reuse of own care data for research without individual consent, with pseudonymisation and role control, and in principle without passing it on. NRW repealed the GDSG NW on 1 July 2025, so older local guidance may be outdated. Doctors in Westphalia-Lippe must see that an ethics committee is consulted for identifiable data. A bill (GeDIG) is pending. This is no legal advice.

Does the AI Act forbid this?

No general ban applies to such tools. High-risk duties moved to December 2027 and August 2028, and pure research systems are exempt. Whether a summariser is a medical device is contested. Search with language processing for a medical purpose is not "simple search".

What do data protection authorities say about retrieval systems?

Such systems can process health data if the legal requirements are met, because the data do not stay in the model. Source references make the input traceable.

Does it hallucinate?

Yes. Stanford estimated 2.33 unsupported claims per chart summary. Omission is at least as important: 46 omissions against 5 hallucinations or errors in one evaluation. Links reveal inventions and hide omissions.

Are local models good enough for German?

For stated facts often yes: Llama 3 70B equalled GPT-4 at 97% on German prostate pathology, while smaller models reached 31 to 83%. For exact rare tumour codes no: 34.7%. Real letters score lower than synthetic ones: procedures 61.3%.

Will we lose skills?

Possibly. After AI was introduced, adenoma detection without AI fell from 28.4% to 22.4%, in a before-after study with other possible causes.

What can I use for my doctoral thesis today?

LangExtract (can run locally, marks the source position), LLM-AIx (local, no code, built-in evaluation), MeDIC extracts, and national data via the research data portal. Always measure on your own sample against a human reading, and clear the data use first.

Do AI scribes save time?

A little: 16 minutes of documentation per 8 patient hours. In one trial one product saved 9.5% and another nothing significant.

Is checking everything not more work than doing it by hand?

Small studies say no: 9.4 to 3.6 minutes, 25.2 to 8.8 minutes. Set-up and validation time is not included. Trials in medicine already pay for checking: source data verification is estimated at 25 to 40% of cost (old estimates).
